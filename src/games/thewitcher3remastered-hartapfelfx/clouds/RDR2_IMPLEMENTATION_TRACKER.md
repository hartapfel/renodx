The talk's primary references include Schneider and Vos (2015), Nubis (2017), Hillaire (2016), Wrenninge et al. (2013), and Annen et al. (2008). This tracks cloud features for the Witcher renderer, not Rockstar's unrelated art assets.

## Acceptance gates

- Preserve native weather transitions, time of day, camera motion, shadow alignment, HDR addon coexistence, and Raster/RT/PT paths.
- In a held sun-through-cloud view, the native disc must obey measured cloud transmission with no later bypass draw.
- Cloud self-shadow remains present; its light-to-dark transitions must be smooth, with no visible step terraces or unstable cloud edges in held and moving views at the default Medium setting.
- Distant clouds must fade into the native atmospheric sky without a straight range cutoff, visible repeating tiles, or a horizon color seam; the ground and reflection views must not acquire far-side clouds.
- The user's coastal dusk captures, preserved for comparison as ignored validation files `tmp/hartapfelfx-validation/coastal-dusk-horizon-full.png` and `coastal-dusk-horizon-crop.png`, are the specific horizon acceptance scene: remove the visibly repeated horizontal cloud pattern and the straight cloud/sky boundary. In the supplied 872x245 crop, the boundary is concentrated in roughly four pixel rows at y=151-155; across x=180-690, 220 of 511 adjacent pixel pairs exceed a combined 24/255 RGB change on row 153, versus zero at row 148. This is a real image discontinuity, not merely a preference for softer cloud shapes. The screenshot predates the current compile-only spherical/deferred-air candidate, so a matched frame is needed to attribute it to the old 80 km cap, the native sky handoff, or repeated weather. Preserve the Witcher's local weather, cloud motion, terrain-shadow alignment, and the user's preferred near-cloud shapes while solving it.
- GPU cloud cost at 1920x1080 Medium must not exceed the loaded baseline in a like-for-like timestamp test; confirm the whole feature cost in game at the same settings.
- Strict DXIL compile, standalone D3D12 cloud suite, addon compatibility host, Release build, then live visual/performance verification.

Both archived fair and rainy b12 frames set `[66].x=5`. With the production weather lookup `uv=(world_xy+wind)*(.0005/[66].x)` and wrap sampling, this repeats the same native weather material every **10 km** in both axes. At a grazing cloud ray reaching roughly 124 km before the 1.2 km base, that periodic input is sampled many times; this is a plausible cause of the reported premeditated raster, though the screenshot has no matched pixel/camera capture to isolate its contribution. The 7.2 km formation noise tile is independent and can reinforce repetition. The user's requirement remains to preserve the native weather *state* and transitions, not to hardcode cloud coverage; any aperiodic tiling experiment must retain layer/blend statistics and one coherent view/shadow/reflection world field. Guerrilla's [Nubis authoring presentation](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2017/Nubis%20-%20Authoring%20Realtime%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20with%20the%20Decima%20Engine%20-%20Final%20.pdf) provides a production weather-map precedent, and [Heitz and Neyret's stochastic texture research](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/738-2/) provides a researched way to remove small-tile repetition; neither justifies changing the Witcher lookup without a matched visual/performance comparison.

Heitz and Neyret explicitly target random-phase or otherwise stochastic texture examples and reconstruct them by blending randomized triangular patches with a histogram-preserving transform. The captured Witcher weather texture is an authored coverage **control** field: its broad spatial fronts may carry game-weather meaning. Applying stochastic tiling blindly to it could move or soften those fronts even if the histogram survives. An aperiodic candidate therefore needs a native-weather spatial-correlation check, not merely a prettier synthetic horizon, and must be evaluated in the common prepass so sun, view, and terrain shadows sample the same field.
